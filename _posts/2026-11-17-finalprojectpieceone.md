The WINTER EYEMASK



The Winter Eyemask Description:
The Winter Eyemask is a snowflake-decorated wearable that allows the wearer to feel like part of the winter wonderland with Frosty. A simple beige eyemask, it is adorned with an abundance of uniquely shaped blue snowflakes and swirling white thread that represents gusts of the cold winter air. In between the eyes, as well, there are two white LEDs that are powered by an attached battery and can be controlled with the flip of a switch, both on the back of the mask. The mask is secured on the face by an adjustable shoelace headband (not pictured), which runs through an eyelet on tabs on either side of the mask. All together, the Winter Eyemask is the perfect complimentary interactive wearable to go along with the Frosty the Snowman Plushy and Blanket.

The Winter Eyemask Motivation:
The Winter Eyemask takes its motivation from the "Homeade Switch Wristband", another Electronic Textiles project that had been completed earlier in the semester. This Eyemask showcases similar skills as that project, however it is also more technically sewn and elaborately decorated, and the switch is just the generic Arduino switch. The creative motivation for this mask came from the Frosty-the-Snowman winter theme and constructing a wearable that allowed the wearer feel closer to Frosty and a part of his winter wonderland. 

The Winter Eyemask Materials List: 
- 2 Pieces of Beige Felt (mask base and overlayed back)
- 1 Piece of Royal Blue Felt (snowflakes)
- 2 White LEDs
- Conductive Thread
- White Thread (accent and attachment)
- Electric Blue Thread (accent and attachment)

Tips to My Past Self:
- My first tip would be to be exceptionally careful with materials. In being less deliberate and thoughtful when measuring out pieces I very nearly ran out of the color of felt I needed, and did run out of the white accent thread, forcing me to switch to electric blue.
- My next tip would be to always sew on a sturdy and flat surface, like a table or desk. I tried to sew from an armchair for a portion of this project and found that it not only produces a less satisfactory product, but also hurts my back from having to bend over.
- My final tip would be to be super deliberate in the order with which I construct parts of the project, as well as sew. I found that I mistakenly sewed forefront details before background designs, and had to go back on my progress or take on the more difficult task of sewing around previously added components. More thoughtfulness in the planning of the order of sewing would have prevented this difficulty and will be a priority for me moving forward.
